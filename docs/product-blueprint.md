1. Executive Summary
1.1 The opportunity
Millions of businesses have poor or fragmented online discoverability. Some have websites that search engines cannot properly understand. Others have no website at all and depend on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, WhatsApp, Shopify, marketplaces, directories or word of mouth. Their business information may be inconsistent, their services are poorly described, their location signals are weak, and potential customers may not find them when searching for what they sell.

The opportunity is to build a platform that helps a business become easier to find, understand, trust and contact across search engines, maps, business directories, social platforms and emerging AI-assisted discovery experiences.

This product must not be another SEO dashboard that only reports problems. It should connect research to execution and measurement.

1.2 Product vision
Make every legitimate business easier to discover by the right customer, in the right location, through the right digital channel.

1.3 Product mission
Turn a business's real-world identity, products, services, locations, reputation and digital assets into a coherent, technically sound and continuously improving online presence—whether or not it owns a website.

1.4 The product in one sentence
A business discoverability and growth operating system that audits, builds, activates and continuously improves a business's search presence across the web.

1.5 The key strategic distinction
Do not compete only on keyword databases, backlink counts or technical audit reports. Established products already provide strong research, auditing, rank tracking and local SEO capabilities. Compete on the connected workflow:

Discover a business and its existing online footprint.
Understand what it offers, who it serves and where it operates.
Research how real customers search for those products and services.
Identify the most valuable visibility gaps and opportunities.
Generate useful, factual, brand-consistent assets.
Implement approved improvements on channels the business controls.
Help the business establish legitimate profiles and listings.
Monitor search visibility, traffic, enquiries and conversions.
Keep improving through an ongoing action plan.
The product should be accessible to a small shop with only an Instagram page and powerful enough to support a multi-location company or a digital marketing agency.

1.6 The commercial promise
We can promise a defined process and deliverables:

A first visibility assessment after onboarding.
A prioritised action plan.
Technical and profile improvements where access is available.
Search-engine submission and discovery setup where supported.
Search-focused content and business information improvements.
Ongoing measurement and recommendations.
We must not promise a number-one ranking, guaranteed indexing, a guaranteed Google Knowledge Panel, a Wikipedia article, or a fixed ranking improvement within 24–48 hours. Search engines control their own crawling, indexing and ranking systems.

A strong positioning line:

We don't just tell you why customers can't find your business. We help build the presence, publish the improvements and measure what changes.

2. Product Principles and Non-Negotiable Realities
2.1 Website optional, structured business identity mandatory
A website is strongly recommended because it gives the business a controllable home for service pages, product information, contact details, structured data, analytics and conversion flows. However, a website must not be a prerequisite for onboarding.

Supported starting states:

Website and active social accounts.
Website only.
Shopify or another commerce platform.
Instagram, Facebook, TikTok or WhatsApp Business only.
Google Business Profile or Bing Places only.
Marketplace/storefront only.
No meaningful online presence at all.
For businesses without a website, the platform should first optimise legitimate profiles and create a recommended website plan. It may offer a hosted one-page business microsite as an optional service. The microsite must contain useful, accurate information and must not be a thin doorway page created solely to manipulate rankings.

2.2 Search ranking cannot be purchased or guaranteed
Google's published guidance explains that organic ranking is algorithmic and that local results are principally influenced by relevance, distance and prominence. The product must not imply that paying a higher subscription buys a better position in Google Search or Maps.

Use language such as:

“Improve your visibility for relevant local searches.”
“Build stronger search foundations.”
“Track where you appear and where opportunities exist.”
“Increase the likelihood that the right customers discover you.”
Avoid:

“Guaranteed number one on Google.”
“Guaranteed first page in 48 hours.”
“Pay us and Google will rank you higher.”
“Guaranteed Wikipedia listing.”
2.3 Indexing is not ranking
The system must explain and report these as separate states:

Published: the asset is live on the website or platform.
Discovered/submitted: a sitemap or URL notification has been submitted where supported.
Crawled: a search engine has fetched the URL.
Indexed: the search engine has included the page in its index.
Eligible/served: the page is eligible to appear for a query and may be shown.
Ranking/visibility: the page appears in a measured result position for a specific query, location, device and time.
Traffic/conversion: a user clicks, calls, messages, visits or purchases.
A successful submission is not proof of indexing. Indexing is not proof of a ranking. A ranking is not proof of a sale.

2.4 Search engine and platform access must be legitimate
Never ask a customer to give us their Google, Meta, TikTok, Shopify or hosting password.
Prefer OAuth, official APIs, approved app permissions or the platform's own owner/manager invitation process.
Use delegated access and least-privilege permissions.
Do not create fake business profiles, fake addresses, fake reviews, false business categories or misleading locations.
Do not scrape search result pages in violation of a provider's terms. Use licensed data providers, official APIs and permitted public data sources.
Make every automated publication, profile edit or destructive technical change subject to clear permissions and, where appropriate, customer approval.
2.5 Content quality over content volume
Do not generate hundreds of nearly identical city pages or keyword-stuffed articles. Google identifies scaled content abuse and doorway pages as spam risks. AI should accelerate useful work, not mass-produce low-value pages.

All generated business claims must be grounded in client-provided or verifiable information. Mark unknown facts for review instead of inventing them.

Module 7 — Technical SEO Deployment Centre
This is where we turn recommendations into verifiable work.

A. XML sitemap
Generate a sitemap from canonical, indexable pages.
Include appropriate lastmod data only when it reflects a real modification.
Exclude redirected, canonicalised-away, noindex and irrelevant URLs.
Split large sitemaps when needed and provide a sitemap index.
Publish at a predictable URL such as /sitemap.xml.
Reference the sitemap in robots.txt where appropriate.
Submit to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools using approved APIs or guided instructions.
Record submitted, processed and error states separately.
B. robots.txt
Detect whether a robots.txt exists at the domain root.
Parse directives and identify accidental blocking of important assets or pages.
Generate a safe recommendation, never overwrite without approval.
Make clear that robots.txt controls crawler access; it is not a reliable way to remove a URL from the search index.
Explain that noindex or access controls are the appropriate controls for exclusion, depending on the use case.
Ensure the sitemap directive is correct if included.
C. Canonical tags
Detect duplicate URLs and canonical conflicts.
Recommend a canonical URL for each indexable page.
Avoid cross-domain canonical changes without expert review.
D. Title and meta description
Generate unique, relevant titles and descriptions.

Do not promise Google will use the exact text; search engines may generate title links/snippets automatically.
